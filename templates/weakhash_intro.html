The threat

MD5 was designed to be fast. That makes it terrible for passwords: attackers who get a leaked database can hash millions of guesses per second and recover the originals. Worse, MD5 hashes used for passwords are usually stored without a salt, so identical passwords produce identical hashes — you can crack many users at once.

Your task

  1. Open the Challenge page — you'll see a leaked dump of usernames + MD5 hashes.
  2. Pick a target user.
  3. Try candidates from the wordlist until one matches the hash.
  4. The lesson completes the moment you find the password.

How this app stores passwords safely

  • bcrypt (cost factor 12) — intentionally slow, ~250 ms per hash. A wordlist attack that finishes in seconds against MD5 takes years against bcrypt.
  • Per-password salt — identical passwords produce different hashes. No more “crack one, crack them all.”
  • Tunable cost — we can raise the cost as hardware gets faster.
